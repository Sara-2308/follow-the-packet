02 / ACROSS THE OCEAN

Most of the internet travels underwater.

Long-distance internet traffic does not bounce across space satellites. Over 99% of intercontinental traffic travels through fiber-optic cables laid directly across the pitch-black ocean floor.

Inside these armored cables resting miles beneath the ocean surface, infrared lasers fire pulses through hair-thin silica glass strands at approximately 200,000 kilometers per second.

03 / THE NETWORK OF NETWORKS

Now the packet enters your ISP.

Your Internet Service Provider (ISP) connects your local city to global backbone transit carriers and peering exchange points.

As your packet arrives at the coastal landing station, it leaves the undersea fiber and enters giant routing computers. These core routers use Border Gateway Protocol (BGP) to examine the destination IP and forward the packet across autonomous networks.
